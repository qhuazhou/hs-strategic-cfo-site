Canadian rental property tax planning

Should you claim CCA on a rental property in Canada?

Capital cost allowance can reduce current taxable rental income, but the decision should also consider loss restrictions, future recapture, sale timing and the owner's wider tax position.

Short answerCCA is not automatically a permanent tax saving. CRA says it cannot create or increase a rental loss, and a later disposition may produce recaptured CCA that is included in income. Compare the current benefit with the possible future cost before claiming it.

Written by Calvin Zhou. Reviewed September 26, 2026. General educational information only—not tax or legal advice.

What CCA does

It spreads the cost of depreciable property over time.

CRA describes CCA as the deduction available over several years for depreciable rental property such as a building, furniture or equipment. Land is not depreciable, and the applicable class and available-for-use rules matter.

The owner can claim less than the maximum available amount, including zero. The decision is therefore partly about timing: reducing taxable rental income now may change the amount exposed to recapture later.

Decision factors

A current deduction should be evaluated against the expected exit.

Rental income and loss limits

CRA says CCA cannot create or increase a rental loss. Calculate the overall rental-income position before assuming a deduction is available.

Expected holding period

A long holding period can make tax deferral more valuable, while a near-term sale may bring the recapture question forward.

Current and future tax position

The value of timing depends on the owner's circumstances, other income, entity structure and future plans—not only the property's standalone result.

Property class and land allocation

The building and qualifying equipment may be depreciable; land is not. Purchase-price allocations should be supportable and professionally reviewed.

Principal-residence considerations

Mixed personal and rental use or change-in-use facts can introduce additional rules. Do not claim CCA without understanding how it interacts with the intended treatment.

Financing and cash reserves

CCA affects taxable income, not mortgage principal, repairs or replacement reserves. The property still needs enough cash to carry its obligations.

What happens on sale

Recapture and capital gain are different calculations.

CRA explains that recapture can arise when disposition proceeds allocated to depreciable property exceed the class's undepreciated capital cost, subject to the applicable rules. A separate capital gain may arise when a property is sold for more than its adjusted cost base and selling costs.

The sale model should therefore include the building and land allocation, undepreciated capital cost, estimated sale proceeds, selling costs, recapture, capital gain and debt repayment.
